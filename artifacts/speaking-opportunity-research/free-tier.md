# Deep research prompt: Cry Me a Free Tier

Copy this entire file into a Deep Research task. This is an instruction brief, not a researched event list.

## Talk to match

- Talk ID: `free-tier`
- Title: Cry Me a Free Tier
- Audience: Engineering leaders, founders, platform teams, FinOps practitioners, and technical product managers.
- Thesis: Expose assumptions tied to today’s AI pricing and evaluate cost per successful outcome, price sensitivity, and dependencies before making durable commitments.
- Available formats: 15, 30, and 40 minutes; 45-minute slot with 5 minutes Q&A.
- Source material: [CFP or outline](../speaking-portfolio-expanded/economics-product/CFP.md)

## Search focus

FinOps; cloud economics; SaaS founder events; engineering leadership; platform engineering; technical product communities.

Seed queries (expand each across years, geography, and submission terminology):

Search FinOps communities, cloud-cost conferences, founder programs, and engineering leadership events. Seek previous sessions on unit economics, credits, pricing risk, and infrastructure dependence. These are search hypotheses, not verified recommendations or current CFP claims.

## Positioning and fit boundaries

The title is provocative. Offer “Building AI Products That Survive the End of the Free Tier.” Avoid asserting that a particular provider subsidizes service or predicting prices. Separate editorial speaking from sponsor-funded placement.

Do not invent Dan’s biography, affiliation, credentials, location, travel willingness, past appearances, or results. Use only supplied speaker material. ## Search method

1. Read the talk description, CFP copy, and outline when accessible. Preserve the thesis and technical depth. If local materials are unavailable, use this brief and record that limitation.
2. Discover candidates through search, professional associations, program archives, conference calendars, Sessionize, Papercall, community chapters, university centers, and organizer sites. Directories and search snippets are discovery leads; follow through to original sources.
3. Run the topic searches below with “call for proposals,” “call for speakers,” “CFP,” “session proposal,” “speaker application,” “program committee,” “webinar,” “meetup,” and relevant year terms. Search adjacent disciplines and regional programs, not only the best-known conferences. Where relevant, try local-language terms and record presentation-language requirements.
4. Find the current edition’s official homepage, dates/location, program tracks, submission page, rules, and organizer. Then inspect up to three past editions. Record past dates and CFP windows only when evidenced. Explain renaming, organizer changes, cancellations, or hiatuses when relevant.
5. Find two specific past sessions or program themes supporting fit for each finalist, including title, speaker if listed, year, and source URL. Similar sessions establish audience appetite but may also show topic saturation. New events can remain candidates if clearly labelled as having no history.
6. Research conference talks and other credible routes: community presentations, association webinars, faculty development, internal guest lectures with public application routes, podcasts with substantive presentation/interview formats, panels, and workshops. Label opportunity type. Workshops require adaptation; the existing deck is not automatically a finished workshop. Distinguish speaker CFPs from paper/poster submissions, attendee registration, sponsor sales, and exhibitor applications.
7. Verify every shortlisted event against its official site and submission system. Check edition/year on every date and link. Record open, announced-not-open, closed, rolling, invitation-only, unknown, cancelled, or no-current-edition-confirmed. A working form alone does not establish that submissions are open.
8. Rank with the rubric below, then produce a short explanation of why each finalist fits, which angle to pitch, what preparation is required, and the next available action. No outreach is authorized.

## Dates and source rules

- Store event start/end dates separately from CFP opening, CFP deadline, notification, speaker confirmation, and materials deadlines. Keep deadline time and time zone verbatim, alongside normalized ISO values only when unambiguous. “Anywhere on Earth” means UTC−12 when explicitly stated. Never assign a midnight deadline or time zone that the source does not give.
- Record exact, tentative, historical, or inferred for each date. A prior edition’s deadline must never appear as the next edition’s deadline. Historical cadence can suggest a monitoring window; label it an estimate and keep it out of confirmed dates.
- When sources conflict, retain both facts and source URLs, flag the conflict, and prefer the current official submission rules over an aggregator. Do not resolve contradictory official pages silently.
- Link directly to the conference, current edition, CFP/form, speaker guidelines, archive, and evidence pages. Keep redirects’ resolved links when useful. Store verification date and a short supporting excerpt or paraphrase for important facts. Missing information is “unknown,” never a guessed URL or date. Record login walls and inaccessible forms.
- Speaker fees, honoraria, travel, hotel, free admission, mandatory registration, workshop revenue shares, visa support, recording rights, exclusivity, accessibility, eligibility, and vendor-neutrality rules require sources. “Not stated” is different from “not offered.” Use public professional organizer contact routes only.

## Ranking

Score each dimension 0–5 and explain the evidence: audience fit (30 points), program/topic fit (25), available submission route and timing (20), format/readiness (10), logistics and economics (10), reach and reuse value (5). Weighted score = sum(weight × rating / 5), out of 100. Reach may use audience composition, archive visibility, and reliable attendance figures; never guess audience size.

Unknown dimensions remain null. Report known-points score and evidence coverage separately; do not turn an unknown logistics preference into a zero or a positive assumption. Compare fully evidenced opportunities first; show high-fit incomplete leads in a separate list. This is a triage score, not an acceptance probability. An unverified deadline cannot support “apply now.”

Use four queues: (1) verified actionable opportunities, (2) upcoming/rolling routes to prepare for, (3) historical fits to monitor for a new cycle, (4) exclusions with reasons. Flag hard barriers such as credential requirements, mandatory paper submission, unavailable format, or sponsorship-only access. A famous name cannot outweigh weak fit.
