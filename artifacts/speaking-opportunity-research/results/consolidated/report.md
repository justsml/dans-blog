## Direct answer

The portfolio has credible near-term speaking routes. The strongest immediate opportunities are concentrated in four clusters:

- Software testing and evaluation: STAREAST, AI Agent Event, EuroSTAR, and Observability Day.
- General developer and product programs: DeveloperWeek, ProductWorld, and GIDS.
- Education and assessment: CHEA, ICAI, UPCEA DT&L, ISTELive, and OLC Innovate.
- Architecture and agent systems: PlatformCon, KubeCon Europe, Cloud Native AI + Inference Day, AOmA, and ICSA's Software Architecture in Practice track.

The ten research lanes produced 244 source event records and 250 talk-to-edition matches. Consolidation resolved those into 148 event series and 158 distinct editions or submission routes, backed by 506 evidence records. Sixty-four editions match more than one talk. Those overlaps are useful, but they create a submission-choice problem: the strongest talk for the audience should lead, with one backup held until the event's multiple-proposal and exclusivity rules are confirmed.

1. **STAREAST is the first go/no-go decision.** Its verified date is September 6, 2026, but the public source did not state a cutoff time or time zone. The `benchmarks` talk is a strong fit; the application also needs a bio, headshot, and marketing consent.
2. **AI Agent Event and CES are next, both dated September 9.** AI Agent Event is the stronger technical fit, but its novelty clause, unstated duration, and preference for real deployment evidence need review. CES is broader and favors visible industry influence; travel and participation are self-funded.
3. **CHEA closes September 11.** Use `evidence-learning` only if Dan can support a quality-assurance or accreditation angle. The program prefers varied perspectives, which may favor a co-presenter.
4. **Resolve the September 18 and September 30 clusters before drafting.** ProductWorld, DeveloperWeek, ICAI, GIDS, UPCEA, EuroSTAR, and ISTELive each impose different audience, evidence, membership, or format constraints. A shared abstract should not be sent unchanged across them.

These are preparation decisions, not authorization to apply.

## Submission-rule constraints

- GIDS requires material grounded in work the speaker directly did or materially contributed to. Do not use a synthetic example as proof of production experience.
- UPCEA requires current membership; corporate members may need an institutional-member co-presenter.
- KubeCon Europe allows at most three proposals, requests a prior recording, and restricts recent Linux Foundation repeats. Its co-located events should not receive near-duplicate proposals without a deliberate selection strategy.
- ICAI allows no more than two submissions bearing one person's name and only one lead program.
- ICSA's practitioner track requires a two-page IEEE-formatted proposal, publication, registration, in-person presentation, and no simultaneous review elsewhere.
- PlatformCon asks for a named implementation and outcomes and does not cover travel. Its official cutoff-time sources conflict.
- Visual Studio Live commonly expects multiple 75-minute sessions; the current talks need either a 20-minute cut or a substantial long-form expansion. Its official deadline time-zone sources conflict.
- ISTELive permits up to five proposals but requires adult presenters to register. The talk needs credible K–12 examples.
- OLC Innovate uses double-blind review, requires presenter registration, and may require an educator or workplace-learning co-presenter for vendor product sessions.

Where simultaneous-submission, repeat-talk, recording, exclusivity, or multiple-proposal rules were not published, the field remains unknown. The portfolio does not recommend submitting a primary and backup to the same edition until those rules are checked.

- Professional memberships, institutional affiliation, education-sector credentials, and role-specific eligibility.
- Permission to name organizations, systems, outcomes, or firsthand production work.
- Existing speaking recordings and audience evidence requested by some programs.

Blank economics fields mean “not stated,” not “not offered.” Invitation-only programs were excluded unless a public professional contact route was evidenced. Paid or sponsored speaking inventory was not mixed into the editorial shortlist; no paid placement was advanced as a recommendation.

## Research limits and stopping point

The researchers used official event sites, official CFP and guideline pages, submission systems reached from organizer pages, and official program archives. Directories, social posts, and search snippets were used only as discovery leads or were labeled when primary evidence was unavailable. The highest-ranked status and deadline pages were rechecked at the end of each lane.

The search stopped when new results were mainly closed editions, paper-only venues, sponsor inventory, undated forms, stale pages, or duplicates. Remaining gaps now require Dan's preferences, biography, permissions, or a future organizer update rather than another broad discovery pass.
