## Scope and decision frame

The search covered academic-integrity, assessment, faculty-development, instructional-design, online-learning, accreditation/quality-assurance, information-literacy, and learning-sciences programs internationally, including virtual routes. It also reviewed the most recent available program or CFP history where discoverable.

The following filters remain unknown and were not silently resolved: travel origin, travel budget, preferred regions, compensation threshold, willingness to pay presenter registration, affiliation and professional memberships, credentials, and availability on event dates. These unknowns are material for several finalists.

The source talk can support 15-, 30-, 40-, and 45-minute slots. Workshop and 50- or 60-minute formats require added interaction; research-conference routes require evidence or scholarship beyond the existing deck.

## Direct result

There are enough credible opportunities to justify immediate preparation. Four official calls are both open and especially relevant: CHEA 2027, ICAI 2027, UPCEA DT&L 2027, and Lilly San Diego 2027. ALA is also open but needs a real academic-library angle. OLC Innovate and UCF Teaching and Learning with AI are top-fit upcoming calls with confirmed opening dates. The best subject match is ICAI. The best virtual fit is OLC Innovate, followed by USDLA's virtual route. The best exact assessment audience is AALHE, but its 2027 CFP was not yet published on the reviewed page, so it belongs in preparation/monitoring rather than the confirmed deadline calendar.

## Fit evidence and saturation

The strongest finalists have two direct program signals where available:

- UPCEA DT&L: 2026 sessions on reframing Bloom's for AI-era assessment and AI-coached training followed by independent assessment.
- OLC Innovate: a 2026 keynote on teaching for integrity and validating learning; a second named session was not visible in the extracted program, so the second record is program-theme evidence and is marked medium confidence.
- UCF: 2024 sessions on authentic assessment in the GenAI era and on AI-supported assessment for deeper understanding.
- USDLA: 2026 sessions on online-proctoring vulnerabilities and assessment design that surfaces reasoning rather than merely policing AI use.
- INQAAHE: 2025 sessions on human evaluators in the AI era and AI-supported programme accreditation assessment.
- AALHE: 2026 sessions on human-centered assessment and assessing learning processes when GenAI can create text.
- Tulane: prior sessions on evidence in course/program assessment and digital citizenship in the age of AI.
- WSCUC: direct organizer programming on AI and authentic assessment plus an ARC2026 assessment/quality-assurance track; one item is not an ARC session and the other is theme evidence.
- Lilly Austin and San Diego: current themes are strong, but two named past-session examples were not verified. They remain shortlisted with lower coverage rather than padded evidence.

The topic is active enough that generic “AI-proof assessment” proposals may look repetitive. The distinctive pitch is the operational evidence sequence: diagnostic question, staged assistance, reconstruction, independent transfer, compact evidence record, and accountable human interpretation. Avoid claims of cheating-proof design or automated misconduct determination.

## Why research stopped

The diminishing-return test was met after 28 distinct series/editions were normalized. Consequential claims for the actionable shortlist have first-party evidence, status and deadline contradictions are either resolved or preserved, at least five regional/community routes were inspected, and another broad search wave was more likely to add low-confidence conference listings than change the recommended order. Remaining gaps are narrow and action-specific: membership/affiliation, presenter economics, live-form fields, organizer provenance for two first-/commercial-edition leads, and unannounced CFPs.
