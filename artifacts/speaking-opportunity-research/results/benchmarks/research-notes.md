## Scope and assumptions

The supplied talk is a 40-minute practitioner session for AI engineers, ML/product engineers, platform teams, CTOs, and technical product leaders. Its core claim is that public benchmarks are useful for shortlisting, while production decisions require a representative workload, observable acceptance criteria, explicit failure classes and severity, a holdout set, a calibrated evaluation ladder, sliced rates with uncertainty, cost per successful task, latency/human-effort measures, whole-system evaluation, and versioned release gates. The included leaderboard values are fictional examples. The talk does not claim research novelty or a deployed evaluation platform.

Travel origin, budget, preferred regions, compensation threshold, willingness to self-fund, biography, affiliation, prior speaking history, production case studies, and eligibility remain unknown. Unknowns are left blank in structured fields and are not scored as favorable.

The required planning helper was unavailable, so the working plan was maintained here: brief and outline intake; broad discovery; official-source verification; gap-focused checks; synthesis; status/deadline recheck; schema and relationship QA. Broad discovery stopped at diminishing returns after 27 routes across 25 series: subsequent results were paper-only, closed, sponsor-led, or lacked a verifiable future edition.
