## 5–10 minute lightning talk (slides)

Seven minutes, four slides: 1, 5, 7, 14.

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–1:00 | Free electricity for a year. Five-second pause. |
| 1:00–2:30 | Shoup's mechanism: hidden price, environment reorganizes, expensive to reverse. |
| 2:30–5:00 | The software map: density, business mix, rent, the small format. |
| 5:00–7:00 | Three prices. Signature line. |

## 5–10 minute live demo (AI Tinkerers format)

The calculator alone is not a "system you built." A qualifying demo runs the sensitivity model against real usage data: pull last month's actual token consumption from a provider dashboard or a Langfuse/Helicone export, compute cost per accepted outcome with a real acceptance rule, and sweep the price live. Five minutes: import, define acceptance, sweep, show which assumption moved the number most. Requires an exported dataset you are permitted to show.

## 60–75 minute workshop

Participants bring one product or feature and leave with its three prices and a sensitivity table.

**Prerequisites sent in advance:** last month's inference spend (or an estimate), a count of attempts and accepted outcomes for one workload, and the current pricing page for the provider in use.

| Time | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Opening | Slides 1–2. Each participant writes their four boxes: what they pay, consume, allocate, deliver. Most leave two blank. |
| 0:10–0:25 | Parking | Slides 5–6. Participants list second-order effects of free parking in pairs, then match each to something in their own stack. |
| 0:25–0:40 | The map | Slide 7. Each participant names one "frontier call where a lookup would do" and one commitment that assumes today's price. |
| 0:40–0:55 | Denominator | Slide 9. Write the acceptance rule for the chosen workload in one sentence. Compute cost per accepted outcome with real or estimated numbers. |
| 0:55–1:10 | Sweep | Slide 10 with the kit. Participants run their own numbers at 1×, 2×, 5×, 10×. Then halve consumption and record the delta. |
| 1:10–1:20 | Counterargument | Slides 12–13. Each participant argues the opposite case for their own product in two sentences. |
| 1:20–1:30 | Three prices | Slide 14. Fill the worksheet. Assign an owner and a review date. |
| 1:30–1:35 | Close | Slide 15. |

**Facilitation notes.** The denominator block is where most participants discover they cannot define an accepted outcome. Allow it to run long and shorten the counterargument block if needed.

**Artifacts participants leave with:** the four-boxes card, the parking-to-software mapping, their sensitivity table, the three-price worksheet.
