## Seven-minute lightning script

This one is a live exercise with a joke on the front and a warning on the back.

0:00 to 1:00, slide 1. The Casio watch and the confession. "I may have used one or two on an assessment. But we're not here for that. What I did was wholesome. These kids are phoning it in." Let it land.

1:00 to 1:45, slide 4. The fixture. $100, up 20%, down 20%, "they cancel." Clean prose, wrong denominator. What would the final answer alone have told you? Nothing.

1:45 to 2:45, slide 5. The smudged sheet. Photograph a hundred problems, make the model do them, and the 8 that reads as a 9 comes back wrong. "You didn't check the results? Johnny, you're not gonna make it." That beats every ban.

2:45 to 5:45, slide 7. The reconstruction, live. Reveal the wrong argument, let the room find the base, $120 only after the diagnostic question, $96 and one sentence. Then $80, up 25%, down 20%, and it comes back to $80. The shortcut the room just formed is gone.

5:45 to 7:00, slide 14. Five verbs. The kid next to the Casio owner didn't need a watch; the kid with no car never touched the clay. Too much power to hold casually. Come find me after.

## Five to ten minute live demo (AI Tinkerers)

The qualifying demo is the smudged-sheet assignment run for real: photograph a prepared arithmetic sheet with one ambiguous digit, submit it to a model, show the wrong answer come back, then show the student-facing prompt handout and an exported session. Second beat if time allows: a tutor session following the hint ladder against a scripted learner, with the four-line record written on screen and the help column filling separately. Requires a model you are permitted to show and a sheet you prepared yourself.

## Sixty-minute workshop

Participants redesign one checkpoint from their own course and leave with a capability statement, a smudge, a hint ladder, a reconstruction task, a rubric, and a record.

**Sent in advance:** one checkpoint (a question, problem, or short task) with its current rubric and the concept it is meant to assess.

| Time | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:10 | Opening | Slides 1 and 2. Participants write the capability their checkpoint assesses in one sentence, then what the artifact could hide. |
| 0:10 to 0:22 | Foundations, fixture, smudge | Slides 3 to 5. Write a plausible wrong answer with clean prose, then the smudge for your subject: the ambiguity that makes the model's error the lesson. |
| 0:22 to 0:35 | Hint ladder | Slide 6. Five rungs for the wrong answer. Pairs test whether each rung leaves the key move to the learner. |
| 0:35 to 0:50 | Reconstruction | Slide 7 at full length, then participants write their own transfer item: same invariant, different numbers or context. |
| 0:50 to 1:02 | Rubric and record | Slide 8. Score the fixture; write the four-line record. Facilitator hunts for labels creeping in. |
| 1:02 to 1:15 | Speaking, AI time, the objective on the board | Slides 9 to 11 compressed. One spoken follow-up per checkpoint; write the objective as the agent's system prompt; estimate minutes to review a class set. |
| 1:15 to 1:30 | Pairs worksheet | Slide 13 at full length. |
| 1:30 to 1:35 | Close | Slide 14. |

**Facilitation notes.** The hint ladder block is where participants discover their hints give away the move. Protect it. If the room skews technical, pair technologists with instructors for the rubric block. The sculptor (slide 12) fits between the worksheet and the close if the room needs it.

**Participants leave with:** the capability card, the smudge, the hint ladder template, the reconstruction and transfer pattern, the rubric, the record, the worksheet.
