## Primary-source check:63 is not per patient

The [full text of Cvach's 2012 review](https://doi.org/10.2345/0899-8205-46.4.268), page 272, reports the earlier project's 15-bed unit,18-day period,16,953 baseline critical alarms and 9,647 afterward. The [original 2010 paper's PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20045845/) reports the 43% reduction and multiple intervention components. The [original paper full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281475704_Standardizing_Progressive_Care_Nurse%27s_Utilization_of_Physiological_Monitors_and_Decreasing_Nuisance_Alarms), Results section, states baseline mean census 12 and postintervention 13.3, with census not included in the count analysis.

Recomputed:16,953÷18÷15=62.7889 alarms per **available bed-day**. This is an explicit calculation using capacity; it is not a patient exposure estimate. The audit's suggested spoken “per patient” substitution is rejected. Also,(16,953−9,647)/16,953=43.0956%. The presentation states a reported multicomponent quality-improvement result, not a causal estimate for deduplication alone and not proof that a software queue will improve. No recommendation about configuring medical alarms is made.
