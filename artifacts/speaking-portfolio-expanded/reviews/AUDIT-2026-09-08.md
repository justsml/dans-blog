## Cross-cutting findings (fix once, everywhere)

### 1. The interaction budget and the word budget share a clock

This is the portfolio's dominant systemic error and it produced blocking findings in **seven of nine talks**. A route's minutes are allocated to spoken words *and* to shows of hands, pair exercises, votes and Story slots, as though the audience talking costs nothing. Every route passes the pacing check until you subtract its own exercises, and then it breaks the 80 wpm ceiling.

| Talk | Where it breaks |
| --- | --- |
| Failure Improvement | 30-min slide 1: 127 words in 60s **plus** a show of hands (127 wpm); 15-min slide 6 at 149 wpm |
| Benchmarks | 15-min slide 9 at 102 wpm once its 45-second exercise comes out |
| Adaptive | 15-min slide 6 at 100.8 wpm; slide 1 at 122 once its scripted 30-second story is subtracted |
| Retrieval | 30-min slide 6 at 92 wpm |
| Product Engineering | 15-min slide 12 at 88.5 wpm |
| Free Tier | All three routes cross 80 once exercises are removed (40-min: 82.4); header says "four exercises", there are six |
| Dynamic Scaling | Both `Story:` slots sit on slides 1 and 2, already the densest at 67/65 wpm; a 50-word anecdote puts slide 1 at 92 |

The voice skill's pacing rule counts spoken paragraphs and bridge lines and excludes demo slides. It has no term for *the audience's* minutes. Add one: every route header should carry a spoken-minutes figure and an interaction-minutes figure that sum to the slot.

## Numbers: wrong, missing, or stated in the wrong form

The portfolio's arithmetic is in much better shape than its *rhetoric about* arithmetic. Three figures are actually wrong; the rest are cases of the right number being dropped, buried, or headlined in an unmemorable form.

**Wrong:**

- **Adaptive `index.md:61`** — "Ten tools is forty-five pairs… hundreds of routes from something the agent can read to something it can do." `C(10,2) = 45` counts read↔read and write↔write pairs, which the sentence explicitly excludes. Under the talk's own 5-read/5-write split it is 25, and after the twelve-endpoint SaaS it is 121 — 96 new, not "hundreds". The correction is also the better argument, because R×W genuinely is a multiplication while `C(n,2)` is not.
- **Dynamic Scaling `index.md:228`** — "a judge reads a thousand tokens and writes fifty, and output is what costs." At every current price point input is ~80% of that judge's bill. Under the talk's own 1/100 monkey premise, five judges cost 131% of the generation they guard. The wrong claim is also rendered into `12-monkeys-then-guards.svg`.
- **Free Tier `index.md:19`** — "Average US ticket that year: $9.11", cited to NATO for 2017. NATO's 2017 average was $8.97; $9.11 is the 2018 figure. In eight files. Relatedly, `index.md:24`'s "eighteen months" is ten (Aug 2017 20k → June 2018 3M), and the correction makes the story better.

**Right number, dropped or buried:**

- **Product Engineering** — 70/22/8 is now **verified verbatim** from the Colfer & Baldwin full text, §4.3, and is exactly the descriptive subset the evidence bank claims. The slide dropped it as a precaution that is no longer needed, and threw away its best hallway number. It also left an orphan: `index.md:29` still rebuts "one third of companies", a figure nothing on the slide states.
- **Retrieval** — the Voorhees–Soboroff–Lin τ > 0.99 the talk deliberately withheld is in the published abstract, for the TREC-8 ad hoc collection. The evidence bank's blocking condition is satisfied; restore it.
- **Outsmart** — has no hallway number. `index.md:46` says detectors flag non-native writers "at alarming rates" where Liang reports 61% of TOEFL essays flagged across seven detectors, near-zero for US 8th-graders, falling to 12% after a native-sounding rewrite. An adjective where a measurement was available.
- **Failure Improvement** — the only number on stage is one the speaker disowns. Cvach's review reports 16,953 high-priority alarms over 18 days on 15 beds, cut 43%: 63 alarms per bed per day. Speaking it also repairs a citation error in the same sentence (`index.md:16` attributes a measurement to what is an integrative review of 72 articles).
- **Judgment** — headlines `32,805 plans per arm`, a quotient nobody can verify by feel. The ratio is the hallway number: half a million release plans, sixteen experiments.
- **Free Tier** — slide 4 says "Everyone reads these as evidence about cost — they are evidence about strategy," then puts $250B on screen in the deck's largest type. The correction does not survive the hallway; the number does.
