# If we drop A Skeptic's Guide: where the content goes

Written 2026-09-06 alongside the [critical review](skeptic-education-review.md). Nothing has been deleted. This is the plan if the decision is yes.

## Why it is droppable

The review measured the overlap with [Outsmart Your Lying, Cheating Students](../outlines/evidence-learning-40min.md): six load-bearing beats duplicate, including Liang, Bastani and TEQSA, plus an identical pairs worksheet at the same slide number in the same minute. Roughly thirteen of forty minutes are genuinely distinct. A program that books both hears the same three citations twice.

The two talks also fail differently. Outsmart has a person in it, a confession, a joke and a checkable number. Skeptic has a framework and no numbers at all. If a committee reads both, they book Outsmart.

## What survives, and where it goes

Most of the value lands in Outsmart, which is the right outcome: it becomes the single strong education talk rather than half of a weak pair.

| Skeptic slide | Destination | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4. Delegate / Augment / Preserve | **Outsmart 3** (write down where the foundations happen) | The highest-value transplant. Outsmart 3 already makes a delegate/preserve decision without naming it. Bring the three columns, and bring the discipline the review found: the assistance dilemma (Koedinger and Aleven 2007) names the problem, and expertise reversal (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler and Sweller 2003) supplies the learner axis both talks currently lack. This is what gives Outsmart the named vocabulary the portfolio standard now expects. |
| 6. The tutor that agrees with you | **Outsmart 6** (ration the help) | Outsmart already asserts "the tutor neither praises the error nor dumps the solution." Skeptic stages it better: Tutor A says "Great point!", Tutor B says "Older than what?" Take the staging. |
| 7. Bastani and Kestin | **Outsmart 8** (score the reasoning) | Outsmart cites Bastani alone, which reads as news. Add Kestin as the counterweight and reframe both with learning versus performance (Soderstrom and Bjork 2015): the dissociation is a fifty-year regularity, not a 2025 headline. That tells the room what to predict about the next twenty tools. |
| 9. Unequal access | **Outsmart 12** (widen the door) | Outsmart 12 is already the equity slide and currently carries only the sculptor story. Skeptic's operational checklist (comparable tools or a usable alternative, accessible ways to explain, permission stated before the task, no surprise surveillance) is the practical half of the same argument. The story and the checklist belong together. |
| 10. The pre-mortem | **Outsmart's 60-minute workshop** | A good workshop activity and not a stage peak. It fits the pairs block in [formats](../packets/evidence-learning/formats.md) better than it fits any slide. |
| 11. Local eval set, four cases | **Stop Looking at My Benchmarks** | Its review found the talk never validates its own instrument. Skeptic's four adversarial cases (a confident wrong answer, a right answer with faulty reasoning, a request for the solution, a learner the tutor misunderstands) are a concrete recipe for building one, in a domain the benchmarks talk does not currently reach. |
| 2. Skepticism is a method | **Stop Looking at My Benchmarks**, opening | "What is the claim? What would count as evidence? Who benefits if we believe it?" is the opening move of a talk about refusing other people's numbers. It is wasted on a talk that then agrees with all four of its own sources. |
| 13b. Vendor questions | **Cry Me a Free Tier** | Its education-adapted abstract already promises "three prices to ask a vendor for." Add the four Skeptic questions: what learning improved, compared with what, for whom, at what staff cost. Plus the demo request, walk me through one learner misconception and show the teaching response. |
| Worked example: the history essay | **Outsmart's demo file**, as an alternate fixture | Outsmart runs on percentages. The two-conflicting-sources essay is the humanities re-skin, which lets the talk be booked by a program with no maths audience. Worth preserving even though no slide uses it today. |
| VanLehn (2011) on two sigma | **Outsmart's evidence bank**, as a Q&A answer | The review's suggested opener. Human tutoring near d=0.79 and intelligent tutoring near 0.76, both less than half the advertised effect. Verify before quoting; the comparison is between heterogeneous meta-analytic medians. It is the ready answer to "but AI gives every kid a personal tutor." |

## What dies, and why that is fine

- **Slide 1 opener.** Outsmart's Casio watch confession is better and already earns the same permission.
- **Slide 3, the proxy is not the capability.** Outsmart 4 makes the identical move with an actual fixture behind it.
- **Slide 5, the demo.** The review called it a lookup table with a dropdown on it. The idea it demonstrates survives inside the new Outsmart 3.
- **Slide 8, detector as judge.** Already Outsmart 2, same source, same conclusion.
- **Slide 12, humans at consequential decisions.** The principle is in Outsmart 8 and is Judgment's whole territory. Keep one line: AI scales instruction far more easily than it scales wisdom.
- **Slide 13a and 14.** Outsmart 13 and 14 are the same exercise and the same landing.

## The one real cost

Outsmart is addressed to an instructor holding one assignment. Skeptic's back half was addressed to whoever runs procurement, and that addressee disappears. Programs that want a strategy session for deans, CTOs or a district office would have nothing to book.

If that matters, there is a cheaper option than deletion: **narrow Skeptic instead of retiring it.** Keep slides 2, 11, 12 and 13b, which are already the procurement talk it keeps trying to become, hand everything else to Outsmart as above, and rebuild it around the economics the Free Tier rewrite already established. That is a genuinely distinct talk with a genuinely distinct buyer, and the overlap problem disappears because the instructor material is gone.
