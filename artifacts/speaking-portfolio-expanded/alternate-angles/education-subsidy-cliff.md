# When the AI School Discount Expires

**Alternate title:** The Tutor Is Free Until the Curriculum Depends on It  
**Best format:** 30–40 minutes; 60-minute procurement workshop possible  
**Audience:** District and university leaders, instructional designers, procurement and curriculum committees, EdTech builders, education policy audiences

## The angle

Schools are being offered cheap or free access to unusually capable AI systems before the sector has settled how to measure learning, safety, dependence, or total operating cost. If those systems prove useful, parents, students, and faculty will reasonably demand continued access. The negotiating moment will arrive after curriculum, support, and expectations have reorganized around the tool.

The talk is not “AI will make school ten times more expensive.” It asks what a school must measure and preserve before a promotional price becomes an institutional dependency.

## Thesis

The right time to design an exit is while the pilot is cheap. Every education AI pilot should produce evidence about learning, a full-cost scenario, and a replacement path—not merely an adoption count.

## Opening

> The dangerous result is not that the AI tutor fails. It is that the tutor works, everyone depends on it, and only then do we learn the durable price.

Give the room two bad choices: remove a tool that now helps students, or fund an input whose future price and terms the institution does not control. Then reject the choice by moving the work earlier.

## Talk arc

### 1. A pilot can hide three bills

- The vendor bill: licenses, usage, storage, support, and future pricing.
- The operating bill: integration, teacher time, review, accessibility, incident handling, and training.
- The dependency bill: curriculum changes, user expectations, accumulated data, and the cost of switching.

### 2. Adoption is not learning

- High usage can coexist with weak independent performance.
- Assisted and unaided outcomes must be recorded separately.
- A classroom control group may be infeasible or inappropriate; local before/after tasks, delayed transfer, and structured qualitative evidence can still improve the decision.
- The school must decide what evidence is strong enough before the pilot starts.

### 3. The success trap

- A useful system becomes politically and pedagogically difficult to remove.
- Parents and students can turn demonstrated benefit into a legitimate demand.
- The institution's leverage is weakest after its curriculum and support model depend on one vendor.

Treat budget growth as scenarios—2×, 5×, 10×—not a forecast. Ask what breaks at each level.

### 4. Buy a capability, preserve an exit

Before adopting, require:

- exportable learner and configuration data;
- local evaluation cases tied to the institution's curriculum;
- an explicit definition of assisted and independent success;
- a replacement test that another model or system can run;
- a plan for smaller, specialist, local, or open-weight models where they meet the evidence bar;
- named owners for safety review, accessibility, and incident response.

### 5. Four questions for the next vendor demo

1. What learning claim could this pilot disprove?
2. What will the institution pay when usage reaches the promised scale?
3. What data, curriculum, and operating work become costly to move?
4. What still works if this model, price, or contract disappears?

Close by asking the room to write the exit condition for one current pilot.

## Audience takeaway

Attendees leave with a three-bill cost model, a learning-versus-adoption distinction, a simple price stress test, and four questions that turn a vendor demo into a defensible institutional decision.

## Relationship to existing talks

**Outsmart Your Students** owns classroom and assessment design. **Show Me the Misconception** owns the four-case local eval and the immediate buying decision. This talk owns the institutional economics after a successful pilot: durable cost, dependence, portability, and bargaining position.

The three can share evidence, but they should not repeat the same classroom exercise. For a procurement-heavy venue, fold Show Me the Misconception into this talk as a 15-minute route rather than submitting both.

## Evidence needed before delivery

- Current terms and pricing for any named education offer.
- At least one sourced example of an education AI pilot's measured outcome, with setting and limitations intact.
- A cited basis for separating assisted performance from durable learning.
- A realistic school or university cost model that includes staff work and accessibility, not just tokens.
- No claim that budgets will `10×`; show it only as one sensitivity row.
- No safety comparison between providers or model origins without task-specific testing.
