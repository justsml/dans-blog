# Name Your Price (No, the Other One)

5 min · lightning · parent: [Cry Me a Free Tier](../outlines/free-tier-40min.md), slides 9, 10, 14

Two of these numbers are on your dashboard. The third decides whether you have a company.

## Hook (0:00)

Three prices. What you pay today. What you'd pay with no promotional treatment. The highest rate at which this product still makes sense. Show of hands: who has computed the third one? That silence is the talk.

## Beat: moral hazard, the org-chart version (0:45)

An engineer picks the architecture in a design review on a Tuesday. Finance sees the consequence sixty days later, in a different building, attributed to nothing in particular. Nobody's behaving badly; the meter is invisible at the moment of choice. Shoup's parking fix was never a ban. It was showing the price to the person choosing.

## Beat: the meter (1:45)

Cost per accepted outcome, in the design doc, next to the latency budget. Define "accepted" as a rule you wrote down, not a vibe. Then run the sweep: 1×, 2×, 5×, 10× on inference price, and separately, move the acceptance rate, and notice it beats the price change.

## Beat: multiply by your volume before you feel anything (3:00)

At a thousand attempts a month this whole exercise isn't worth an afternoon. At a million it's a hiring plan. The most expensive mistake available in the room isn't overspending on inference; it's three weeks optimizing a thirty-dollar bill because a talk made you anxious. Sweep downward too, so you can cancel the optimization project when efficiency makes it pointless.

## Landing (4:15)

Four actions. Record gross usage separately from credits. Keep a small acceptance suite. Rehearse one provider replacement before you need it. And write the third price down, because "what can we survive paying" is the only version of this argument that ends a budget meeting.

## On screen

Three price tags on strings, the highest one singed. Then the four-row sweep table from the parent talk.

## Scope

Fixture arithmetic, synthetic. No provider margin is estimated.

## Source
