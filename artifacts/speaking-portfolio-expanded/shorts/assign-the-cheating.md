# Assign the Cheating

3 min · video / lightning · parent: [Outsmart Your Lying, Cheating Students](../talks/evidence-learning/outline-40min.md), slide 5

The highest-leverage assignment I know lets students feel like they're getting away with something.

## Hook

"Photograph these hundred problems. Feed them to the model. Submit whatever comes back." Teachers in the room just flinched. Good.

## Beat: the trap is in the sheet

A 1 that reads as a 7. A smudge that turns an 8 into a 9. The model does exactly what it was told, the wrong answers come back, and the lesson teaches itself: you were supposed to check. "You didn't verify the results? Johnny, you're not gonna make it." That one experience beats every ban, filter and lockdown, and nobody got in trouble.

## Beat: scale it up

Hand out the prompt like a worksheet. Older students get experimentation as the objective: the writing challenge, the rhyming challenge, whatever riddles the English teachers love. The constraint that keeps it honest is personalization. Narrate it from summer camp. Reference this week's unit. Meet a criterion the model can't know. And privacy rides along for free: sure, mention Lake Tahoe, maybe not the photo of the cabin any model can geolocate.

## Landing

Nobody is teaching students how these systems actually behave. This assignment does, and it does it by letting them find out the machine was wrong before the grade did.

## On screen

A phone photographing a dense worksheet. Zoom on one smudged digit. Caption: *what would the smudge be in your subject?*

## Story slot

The submission where the grade and the understanding first came apart.
