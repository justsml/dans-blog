# Free Parking Cost You a City

3 min · video / lightning cold open · parent: [Cry Me a Free Tier](../outlines/free-tier-40min.md), slide 5

The least glamorous subject in urban economics, and it's your inference bill.

## Hook

Cities required developers to build minimum parking. Nobody paid at the meter. So where did the cost go?

## Beat: the cost relocated

Into rents. Into retail prices. Into land that became asphalt. And because drivers saw no price, demand looked infinite, so the minimums grew to match. That's word one: externality. A cost that is real and paid, just not by the person deciding. It doesn't vanish. It moves somewhere you're not looking.

## Beat: the environment reorganized

Buildings spread apart. Formats that need a big lot won; the storefront that couldn't fit one never opened. Every apartment carried a parking space paid for by tenants who don't drive. Transit lost. Every step was locally reasonable. Then cities repealed the minimums and the lots stayed built.

## Landing

Now swap "parking" for "inference." Frontier call where a lookup would do. Unlimited-usage clause in a contract. Deterministic code losing the design review to "just ask the model." Nobody's wrong. Nobody saw a meter. Shoup's fix was never a ban; it was charging the right price and showing it to the person choosing.
