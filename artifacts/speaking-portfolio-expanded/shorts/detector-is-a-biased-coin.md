# Your AI Detector Is a Coin That Hates Second-Language Writers

2 min · video · parent: [Outsmart](../outlines/evidence-learning-40min.md) slide 2 · [Skeptic's Guide](../outlines/skeptic-education-40min.md) slide 8

It flags the ESL kid, misses the one with a VPN, and even when it's right it's answering the wrong question.

## Hook

The detector says 87% AI-generated. What do you actually know?

## Beat: what the evidence says

Liang and colleagues tested the detectors on the market and found them flagging non-native English writers at alarming rates, and easily fooled by a rewrite. That's evidence about tested tools, not a prophecy about every future detector. It's enough to say: no disciplinary consequence gets automated from a score.

## Beat: the wrong question

Suppose the detector were perfect. Who typed the sentence is still not whether the kid can do the thing. An authorship inference is not learning evidence, and learning evidence is not a misconduct decision. Three different questions; the detector answers the least useful one, badly.

## Landing

Stop trying to prove who typed it. Ask whether the student can explain it, challenge it, change it, apply it and defend it. That's a design problem, and it's yours, not the vendor's.
