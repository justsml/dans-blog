# Evidence: Benchmarks

Sources checked 8 September 2026 are linked beside each slide. This talk owns instrument validity, repeatability, judge agreement, and small-sample inference. Free Tier owns cost per accepted outcome. Retrieval owns the detailed IR history and pooling exercise.

All model scores, costs, and score sequences are teaching fixtures. The published judge article supplies the voice and examples, not a new measured run. Its threshold example is corrected here: fail, fail, pass, pass, pass gives 40% majority disagreement and one adjacent transition.

Twenty independent representative trials with zero failures give an exact one-sided 95% upper bound of 13.91%, approximated by 3/n = 15%. A handpicked corpus does not support that population bound. About one in seven communicates scale, not an estimated failure rate.

Story: the green eval followed by a production failure. Supply the original case, scorer version, score, production observation, and permission to name the system. No personal incident was invented.

Goodhart (1975), Campbell (1979) and Strathern (1997) appear as attributed paraphrases rather than quotations, with Strathern credited for the familiar wording. The argument is about pressure on a target; it does not claim that every optimized benchmark has ceased to measure anything.

## Verification and scope, 8 September 2026

[Hanley and Lippman-Hand’s author-hosted reprint](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/c607/ch08/zero_numerator.pdf), pp. 2–3 and Table 2, gives the exact zero-event equation and the 3/n approximation. Recomputed: 1 − 0.05^(1/20) = 0.13910834066826516; 3/20 = 0.15. One in seven is 14.2857%, slightly above the exact bound. It is a rounded scale description, not literally an unexcluded rate or an estimate. The audit’s proposed CFP wording repeated that imprecision; the corrected abstract uses 13.9% explicitly.

[Shi et al.’s published paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18/) is IJCNLP-AACL 2025, pp. 292–314; use this version instead of the 2024 preprint title. [Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819) supports self-preference in the tested setting, not a universal bias rate. [Feinstein and Cicchetti](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2348207/) explains why high agreement and low kappa can coexist. The 90/10 toy matrix is our arithmetic, not a reported experiment.

Cronbach and Meehl’s [1955 article](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Cronbach/construct.htm) requires a network of theoretical relationships for construct validation. The cancellation fixture instead has observable criteria; it does not claim to validate general intelligence. [Messick](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/1990/ihmy.html) centers interpretation and use; [Raji et al.](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/084b6fbb10729ed4da8c3d3f5a3ae7c9-Abstract-round2.html) applies construct-validity concerns to broad benchmarks. The canonical page was search-index accessible but blocked direct retrieval in this check; no claim of a newly read complete Cronbach text is made.

[Card et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.745/) supports the power warning. A zero-event interval is an uncertainty calculation, not itself a power analysis of a two-system comparison. [Oren et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/46e624c244cff669223d488defd4e835-Abstract-Conference.html) makes exchangeability an assumption of its contamination test; [Dwork et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26250683/) studies valid adaptive holdout reuse. Neither says every public benchmark is contaminated.

The [RBA bibliography](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/1990/9013/conference-volumes.html) records Goodhart’s contribution to the July 1975 conference. Campbell’s [journal-hosted reprint](https://jmde.journals.publicknowledgeproject.org/index.php/jmde_1/article/download/297/292/988) and Strathern’s [paper](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1997-strathern.pdf) support their distinct attributions. The Cohen publisher endpoint blocked automated retrieval; the DOI and the explicit formula remain the reference, not a claimed full-text reread.
