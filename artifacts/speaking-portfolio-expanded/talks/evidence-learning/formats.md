## Seven-minute lightning script

This one is a live exercise with a joke on the front and a warning on the back.

0:00 to 1:00, slide 1. The Casio watch and the confession. "I may have used one or two on an assessment. But we're not here for that. What I did was wholesome. These kids are phoning it in." Let it land. Keep the integrity process; these are authored examples and a proposed rubric.

1:00 to 1:45, slide 4. The fixture. $100, up 20%, down 20%, "they cancel." Clean prose, wrong denominator. What would the final answer alone have told you? Nothing.

1:45 to 2:45, slide 5. The smudged sheet. Photograph a hundred problems, make the model do them, and the 8 that reads as a 9 comes back wrong. "You didn't check the results? Johnny, you're not gonna make it." That rehearses checking.

2:45 to 5:45, slide 7. The reconstruction, live. Reveal the wrong argument, let the room find the base, $120 only after the diagnostic question, $96 and one sentence. Then $80, up 25%, down 20%, and it comes back to $80. The shortcut the room just formed is gone.

5:45 to 7:00, slide 14. Five verbs. The kid next to the Casio owner didn't need a watch. Too much power to hold casually. Come find me after.

## Five to ten minute live demo (AI Tinkerers)

The proposed qualifying demo is the smudged-sheet assignment run for real: photograph a prepared arithmetic sheet with one ambiguous digit, submit it to a model, inspect the answer that comes back; if it reads the ambiguous digit correctly, use the prepared error as a clearly labeled fallback, then show the student-facing prompt handout and an exported session. Second beat if time allows: a tutor session following the hint ladder against a scripted learner, with the four-line record written on screen and the help column filling separately. Requires a model you are permitted to show and a sheet you prepared yourself.

## Sixty-minute workshop

Participants redesign one checkpoint from their own course and leave with a capability statement, a smudge, a hint ladder, a reconstruction task, a rubric, and a record.

**Sent in advance:** one checkpoint (a question, problem, or short task) with its current rubric and the concept it is meant to assess.

| Time | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:05 | Opening | Two minutes framing; three writing the target capability and what the artifact hides. |
| 0:05 to 0:13 | Reconstruction | Four-minute demonstration on slide 7; four minutes writing a transfer item. |
| 0:13 to 0:25 | Smudge and hint ladder | Four-minute explanation of slides 5 and 6; eight minutes drafting and testing help rungs in pairs. |
| 0:25 to 0:35 | Rubric and record | Three-minute explanation; seven minutes scoring the fixture and recording support separately. |
| 0:35 to 0:43 | Access and staff time | Two-minute setup; six checking devices, language, written alternatives, retention, and a class-set review budget. |
| 0:43 to 0:53 | Pre-mortem | Two-minute setup: the pilot failed after six weeks. Four minutes naming a failure, two choosing an early signal, two assigning a stop/revise decision and owner. |
| 0:53 to 0:58 | Peer challenge | One-minute instructions; four testing a neighbor's hidden assumption against their checkpoint. |
| 0:58 to 1:00 | Close | Two-minute synthesis and one next teaching decision. |

**Facilitation notes.** The hint ladder block is where participants discover their hints give away the move. Protect it. If the room skews technical, pair technologists with instructors for the rubric block. If using the sculptor story, substitute it for the access block setup; do not add time.

**Participants leave with:** the capability card, the smudge, the hint ladder template, the reconstruction and transfer pattern, the rubric, the record, the worksheet.
