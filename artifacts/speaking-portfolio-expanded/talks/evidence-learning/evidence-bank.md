## Claims we deliberately do not make

- All AI detectors are useless, or all tools share one accuracy figure.
- A detector score, a polished artifact, or a replay proves either misconduct or mastery.
- Prompt instructions guarantee pedagogical behavior.
- Oral defense, local data, process logs, or adaptive questioning prevent cheating.
- A short stage exercise measures a durable learning benefit.
- Tutor engagement, completion, and satisfaction are substitutes for independent learning evidence.
- Delegate, Augment, and Preserve are permanent categories of activities. They depend on objective, learner, and stage.

## Proposed designs, not measured findings

The percentage fixtures, six-point rubric, hint ladder, compact record, sampling plan, and human escalation rules are authored demonstration materials. Their purpose is to make assessment choices inspectable. Before consequential use, align them with local objectives, calibrate scoring, include learners and accessibility expertise, and measure staff burden. No empirical success rate is asserted.

## Q&A: “Doesn't AI give every kid a personal tutor?”

A tutoring interface is not a measured tutoring effect. [VanLehn (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369), Educational Psychologist 46(4), 197–221, reports mean effects of d = 0.79 for human tutoring and 0.76 for step-based intelligent tutoring systems against no tutoring. These are means across heterogeneous comparisons, not medians and not a direct equivalence trial. They do not measure modern general-purpose chatbots. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.public.asu.edu/~kvanlehn/Stringent/PDF/EffectivenessOfTutoring_Vanlehn.pdf).

Bloom's 1984 two-sigma result remains a historical result under particular tutoring and mastery-learning conditions. The later review does not justify promising every learner a 2.0-standard-deviation gain; it does not imply Bloom fabricated data. Ask what this tutor made these learners do and what they could do after it stopped helping.
