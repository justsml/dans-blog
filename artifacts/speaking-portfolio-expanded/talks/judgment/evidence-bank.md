# Evidence: Turn Your Thinkin' Tokens Up to 11

Audited claims were refreshed 8 September 2026 and are attached to the canonical outline. This bank records what each one does *not* establish.

**Feature fatigue.** Thompson, Hamilton and Rust (2005) is consumer-product research: capability dominates choice before use, usability dominates satisfaction after use. It is not a study of software release cadence, and the inference to SaaS is mine. Kano et al. (1984) is a classification model, widely applied and rarely validated as a predictive instrument; the decay of delighters into expectations is the model's claim, not a measured longitudinal result.

**The cost of change.** Samuelson and Zeckhauser (1988) and Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler (1990) are decision-research results about ownership and defaults, not about user interfaces. Nielsen's recognition-over-recall heuristic is a design heuristic, not an experiment. Memory decay across a session gap is real — see Murre and Dros (2015), *Replication and Analysis of Ebbinghaus' Forgetting Curve*, PLoS ONE 10(7) — but that work uses nonsense syllables and a single subject, so it supports the direction of the effect and nothing about its size in a product.

**The two tracks.** The absorption diagram is an illustration with internally consistent arithmetic: 46 releases across 90 days on roughly an every-other-day cadence, sessions on days 3, 19, 61 and 88, so exactly 21 releases fall in the 19-to-61 gap. It is not telemetry from any product. DORA's four keys (Forsgren, Humble, Kim, 2018) measure delivery performance; the claim that they do not measure user absorption is a statement about their definitions, not a criticism of the research.

**The plan-space arithmetic.** 720 × 729 = 524,880 is exact. The per-arm figure uses n ≈ 16·p(1−p)/δ², a standard sizing heuristic approximating 80% power at α = 0.05; it is not a derivation and the talk says so. Benchmarks owns instrument validation and small-sample inference.

**Reasoning budgets.** Chollet (2019) is a definition and a benchmark proposal, not a measurement of any current model. The *Illusion of Thinking* exchange is presented as contested: the Apple paper reports collapse past a complexity threshold and declining token spend near it; A. Lawsen's unrefereed comment (arXiv:2506.09250v2) argues that output-token limits and one unsolvable River Crossing instance account for much of it. Deliver both, take neither as settled. The product claim does not depend on either result: more reasoning tokens do not create additional customer observations.

**Predictions.** Slide 1 states the overall scope; slides 10 and 12 identify the first and second predictions. No adoption figure, no company, no timeline is asserted. The privacy-versus-patching trade on slide 11 is an engineering argument, not a measured comparison. Hyrum's law is an observation about interface consumers with no study attached.

**Choice overload.** Iyengar and Lepper (2000) is cited in order to be set aside: Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd (2010) meta-analyzed 63 conditions from fifty published and unpublished experiments and found a mean effect near zero. Do not use the jam study as support for anything on stage.

**Unfilled stories.** Slide 1: the feature you were proudest of that a long-absent customer met as a broken workflow — bring the ticket, the session gap, and their words. Slide 7: the release you pulled forward to answer a competitor — bring what slipped, what support absorbed, and whether it worked. Both remain unfilled; no personal anecdote was invented.

**Audience and boundaries.** Product engineers, engineering and product leaders, founders, and anyone who owns a release calendar. Product Engineering owns coordination cost, agent interfaces and human ownership inside the org; this talk stays out of the org chart and owns the decision about what reaches users and when. Adaptive owns runtime repair and per-job agent generation; the personalization prediction here is about a user-facing configuration surface, not about an agent rewriting the app at runtime. Free Tier owns cost per accepted outcome.

## Verification and interpretation, 2026-09-08

[Lawsen v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09250v2) uses the title *Comment on The Illusion of Thinking: Understanding the Strengths and Limitations of Reasoning Models via the Lens of Problem Complexity*, with A. Lawsen as author. [Version 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09250v1) also lists C. Opus; the revision note says Claude was removed under arXiv policy and mistakes were corrected. It is an arXiv comment, not a refereed rebuttal. The talk summarizes its output-limit and impossible-instance objections, not a settled conclusion about reasoning. [Apple's research page](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/illusion-of-thinking) supports the other side of that exchange. [Chollet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547) defines skill-acquisition efficiency with priors and experience; a definition is not evidence about a particular current model.

[The choice-overload paper](https://www.oxpal.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf) reports 63 conditions from 50 published and unpublished experiments, N=5,036, with a near-zero mean and considerable heterogeneity. That does not establish no overload in this product. [Current DORA documentation](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/) includes throughput and instability measures; the talk explicitly names the 2018 four-key model historically. Deployment frequency alone is not a health verdict, and DORA does not prescribe forcing interface changes on every returning user.

[contracts.md](contracts.md) now states all plan-space and sample assumptions. Twelve thousand WAU repeated four times is not 48,000 independent users. The fixture instead assumes 12,000 new eligible users each week. Sixteen arm slots include a control and are not sixteen fully powered experiments. The two-arm normal heuristic is optimistic for a multi-arm family; verify variance, multiplicity, assignment unit, attrition and control allocation before using it. The plan count does not require testing every candidate independently and does not prove that analytics or models are useless.

**Prediction evaluation.** No invented adoption deadline or top-five market denominator is attached to Dan's forecasts. These remain directional predictions, not calibrated forecasts. To test the product mechanism, pre-register task-completion, support burden and patch-compliance outcomes before a bounded version-choice pilot, compared with a stable supported default. Log pin reasons: fatigue, compatibility, habit and training schedules are competing explanations. A version picker that adds choice without improving user outcomes weakens the product case. For shared profiles, compare discoverability, task completion, unsafe settings and support burden against curated defaults; profile downloads alone do not establish value. Those are proposed tests, not results.

**Security boundary.** Per-tenant workers can reduce cross-tenant exposure only if credentials, storage authorization and egress are scoped. A compromised shared control plane, backup or export path can still affect many tenants. Version choice must have support windows and mandatory security-update policy; a literal sandbox per user is one architecture, not a prerequisite for every version picker.

**Editorial decisions.** The existing thesis survives: the user's relearning cost is the decision. The version-choice hinge keeps supported options, defaults and patch policy with the product owner while letting users supply preference feedback. Pinning is not a validated fatigue instrument. Slide 9 remains a short demonstration of useful model assistance and accountability, with its defensive opening removed; it is not a detour into an anti-AI argument. First-hand Story substitutions are limited to 40 existing words / 25 seconds on full routes and omitted on the 15 route. No customer record or measured outcome was invented.
