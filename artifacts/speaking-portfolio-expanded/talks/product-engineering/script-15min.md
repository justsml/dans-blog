# Break the Mirror on Purpose: 15-minute presenter script

Use slides 1, 3, 5, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17. [Timing](timing.md) includes all audience work. The scorecard is authored policy arithmetic, not a live experiment. Keep candidate labels neutral until the vote; name Campbell only afterward. Optional stories replace rehearsed prose.

## 00:00 to 01:45: slide 1, The architecture you could read off the org chart

> You design the agents; your org chart chooses their handoffs.
> Conway, 1968: communication structures constrain designs

You design the agents; your org chart chooses their handoffs. Unless you make those handoffs a decision too.

Conway's 1968 argument connects system design with communication structure. Research writes a brief; product translates it into tickets; support translates the software back into complaints. Which translations carry judgment, and which copy a sentence between tools?

This is a proposed operating model with a synthetic policy exercise. Mirroring has empirical support and exceptions; hidden technical dependencies make premature modularization dangerous. Price a handoff before removing it.



## 01:45 to 03:25: slide 3, Who Ordered This Handoff?

> Coase, 1937: coordination has a cost
> Reprice the handoff before copying it

Coase's 1937 comparison concerns firms and markets, not deleting departments. My application is narrower: an agent may make collecting and transmitting evidence cheaper, while verification and maintenance get more expensive.

A weekly brief may need less preparation. The decision meeting may still earn its cost. Compare the work removed with the checking, permissions and recovery added. Start with the outcome and the handoff, not one agent per job title.



## 03:25 to 05:20: slide 5, Draw the wires between agents

> Research → sourced brief → product review
> Feedback → cited clusters → product review
> Review → accepted hypothesis → build

Take one wire: feedback sends cited clusters to product review. What arrives, what leaves, who decides, and what did it cost last week?

An hour saved collecting evidence plus two hours correcting it is a worse trade. Research and feedback can disagree; keep the meeting that resolves that disagreement. Its output is an accepted hypothesis, not six chat histories for a build agent to reconcile.

Draw the artifact contract before assigning agents. An agent roster without the wires is a seating plan.

## 06:10 to 09:10: slide 11, Open the Rest of the Record

> A · 40% activation
> B · 48% activation
> C · 45% activation

A is forty percent activation, B forty-eight, C forty-five. Vote first.

Now reveal: B has nine percent support against a five-percent ceiling, and fabricated urgency. Raising the ceiling to ten still leaves it blocked. C has forty-five percent activation, four percent support and no false urgency: eligible for review, not shipped.

Write your rule. Compare it with the policy saved before the scorecard. A metric win cannot buy off a product principle.

Delivery: Twenty-second vote; twenty-second reveal reading; ten-second silence; thirty seconds writing; ten-second comparison. Name Campbell on the next slide.

## 09:10 to 11:05: slide 12, Where guards go, including the two we forgot

> Wider rollout · expensive runs · infrastructure changes
> Customer messaging · customer data deletion

Campbell names what decision pressure can do to an indicator. Put guards before consequences: wider exposure, spend, infrastructure changes, customer messages and deletion. A person approves the consequential action; the tool layer enforces it.

Two predictions close this talk. First, tokens become ongoing fuel for software and its defensive agents; price the burn and name its owner. Second, generated interfaces could change the software channel: the assistant through which people find and use tools. Who gets to own that channel?



## 11:05 to 13:35: slide 15, Minority Report, With Terminals

> Generated UI, adapting in real time, per person
> Yes, the software you mastered is going away. As you know it.
> The channel is not spoken for.

Second prediction: people use an interface generated for their task, adapting in real time, instead of choosing every tool off a shelf. Normal people do not want to vibe-code a recipe tracker. They may accept a tool generated for them.

That could look like Minority Report, with a surprise revival of the terminal. Skynet is not so bad if I get to keep my CLIs.

The opportunity belongs in this room. GPT image being good does not mean image creation belongs to the big players. Builders outside frontier labs can rethink every app as an AI-native system. The channel is not spoken for.

Watch repeated use. A generated interface that impresses once but never replaces a daily task would weaken the prediction.

Delivery: Take one fifteen-second answer naming a daily product someone could rethink. Do not resolve it.

## 13:35 to 15:00: slide 17, Break the mirror on purpose

> Reprice the handoffs. Draw the interfaces.
> Automate the right things. Keep the taste.

Return to the handoff. What arrives, what leaves, who decides, and what did it cost last week? Price the verification, ownership and fuel alongside the saving.

Your org chart can choose the handoffs for you, or you can make them explicit. Product categories are inherited choices too; builders outside frontier labs can redraw those boundaries.

Break the mirror on purpose.

Delivery: Return to the opening handoff. Stop talking.
