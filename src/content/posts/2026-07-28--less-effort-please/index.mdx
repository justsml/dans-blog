AI products have trained us to turn every knob clockwise.

More context. More tools. More tokens. More thinking.

<blockquote class="inset">
  Apparently intelligence is a guitar amp. Let's turn it up to 11!
</blockquote>

While testing [ExploitHunter.app](https://github.com/justsml/ExploitHunter.app), I ran GPT-5.6 Sol through the same browser-based XSS hunt at every supported thinking level: `none`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, and `xhigh`.

I expected a tidy staircase. More thinking would buy more quality and cost more time.

The staircase was apparently built by M.C. Escher.

## Same model, same hunt, five personalities

These are route-level results: a refusal counts as a failed run, because in production it does not become successful merely because a spreadsheet politely excludes it.

| Thinking | Answers returned | Judge | Runtime | Cost | Tool calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `none` | 3/3 | **100%** | 278s | $0.316 | 11.0 |
| `low` | 2/3 | 64.2% | 103s | **$0.144** | **3.3** |
| `medium` | 2/3 | 65.3% | **91s** | $0.177 | 4.3 |
| `high` | 2/2 | 97.9% | 99s | $0.169 | 4.0 |
| `xhigh` | 3/3 | 95.5% | 92s | $0.172 | 4.3 |

Yes, `none` received full marks.

It also took three times as long as `xhigh`, used two and a half times as many tool calls, and burned nearly three times as many tokens.

We did not remove thinking. We moved it into the tool loop and paid retail.

<p class="inset">
Turn thinking off and the model may start thinking out loud with your infrastructure.
</p>

## "None" is not nothing

Without internal reasoning, Sol still found its way to strong answers. It just wandered there through repeated browser actions, observations, and course corrections.

That route was accurate. It was also operationally absurd.

Token accounting tends to miss this. Reasoning tokens have a visible price, so they look expensive. Hesitation hides in tool calls, screenshots, retries, larger context, and wall-clock time. Apparently waste becomes sophisticated once it has several billing columns.

`low` and `medium` found another optimization: refusing one of three runs. Their successful answers were good. Their actual routes succeeded two-thirds of the time. An omitted result remains wonderfully cheap right up until someone wanted the result.

Meanwhile, `high` and `xhigh` occupied the useful middle: roughly the same runtime, cost, and tool count, with strong answers. `high` edged `xhigh` on quality, though it has only two unique replacement runs. Promising, not canonization material.

There is no monotonic ladder here. There is a behavioral switchboard.

## Optimize indecision

Thinking effort is not a quality slider. It changes where the model searches, how often it acts, whether it refuses, and when it decides the job is finished.

So measure the whole route:

- Did it answer?
- Was the answer good?
- How long did the investigation take?
- What did it spend while visibly "not thinking"?

The cheapest reasoning setting was not the cheapest reliable agent. The highest setting was not the highest-scoring one. And `none` was less a lack of thought than an expensive public performance of it.

Do not minimize reasoning tokens. Minimize total indecision.

Less effort, please.
